What Is ATS and Why Should You Care?

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software used by employers and recruitment agencies to automatically process, sort and filter job applications at scale. When you submit a CV online, it often goes into an ATS before any recruiter sees it.

The ATS does two things:

  1. Parses your CV — extracts your contact information, work history, education and skills from the document's text
  2. Scores your application — compares the extracted content against the job requirements and keywords in the listing, then ranks applications

If your CV can't be parsed correctly (due to formatting issues) or doesn't contain the right keywords, it gets filtered out. A recruiter may never see it.

What Makes a CV "ATS-Friendly"?

ATS compatibility is about two things: formatting the system can read, and content the system can match. Both matter.

ATS-Safe Formatting

  • ✓
    Single-column layout — easy for ATS to parse top-to-bottom without confusion about reading order
  • ✓
    Standard section headings — "Work Experience," "Education," "Skills" — headings ATS recognises
  • ✓
    Plain text fonts — standard fonts like Arial, Calibri, Georgia — no decorative fonts that may not render
  • ✓
    Bullet points using standard characters — plain dashes or dots, not decorative unicode symbols some ATS can't parse
  • ✗
    Tables to lay out content — ATS often reads across table rows rather than down columns, scrambling your data
  • ✗
    Text in headers, footers or text boxes — many ATS skip content outside the main body
  • ✗
    Logos, profile photos or graphics inside the body text — images are invisible to ATS
  • ✗
    Two-column layouts for experience sections — ATS may merge the two columns or skip one entirely

ATS Keyword Optimisation

Even a perfectly formatted CV can fail ATS if it lacks the right keywords. Here's how to get them right:

  • Read the job description and identify repeated terms. If "stakeholder management" appears three times, your CV should include that exact phrase.
  • Use the exact terminology in the listing. If the job says "Python" and your CV says "programming," the ATS may not make the connection.
  • Include both acronyms and full forms. Write "Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)" once, then use "SEO" freely — covers both search patterns.
  • Don't keyword-stuff. Modern ATS systems look for contextual relevance, not raw keyword frequency. Use keywords naturally within your bullet points.

The ATS Myth: "ATS Rejects Creative CVs"

There's a misconception that any coloured, designed or visually interesting CV will fail ATS. This is not entirely accurate. Modern ATS systems (especially cloud-based ones like Greenhouse, Lever and Workday) have improved significantly and can handle colour, simple design elements, and standard fonts.

What actually causes ATS failures in 2026:

  • Multi-column layouts where the ATS reads across rows rather than down columns
  • Content in text boxes or headers/footers that the ATS parser ignores
  • Unusual section headings the ATS doesn't recognise (e.g., "My Story" instead of "Work Experience")
  • Submitting a PDF that was scanned from a physical document (image-only PDF, no selectable text)
  • Missing keywords — the most common cause, and the most fixable

ATS Resume Checklist Before You Submit

  • CV uses a single-column template (or you've verified the employer uses ATS-compatible parsing)
  • All experience and education is in the main body, not headers or footers
  • Section headings match expected labels: Work Experience / Education / Skills / Certifications
  • You've read the job description and included key terms verbatim where relevant
  • Both acronym and full form are written out at least once for technical terms
  • Contact information is in the main body, not just in the document's header
  • The PDF contains selectable text (not a scanned image)
