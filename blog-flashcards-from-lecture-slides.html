How to Make Flashcards From Lecture Slides (Free)

By Pritom Dash · Published November 4, 2026

How to make flashcards from lecture slides?

Lecture slides are mostly headings and fragments. How to turn them into flashcards that actually test you — by hand or with a free AI tool — and what to check.

Every lecturer I had uploaded the slides after class, and every exam season I opened forty decks of bullet points and felt like I knew nothing. Re-reading slides feels productive and isn’t. Turning them into questions is what made revision work for me.

Why slides make poor revision material on their own

Slides are prompts for a talk, not the talk. “Krebs cycle — 3 stages” tells you nothing you can be tested on. A flashcard forces the missing part: What are the three stages of the Krebs cycle, and what does each produce? Answering that from memory — active recall — is what makes it stick.

How do I turn slides into flashcards?

  1. Pick one slide deck per session. One lecture, one deck.
  2. Turn each heading into a question. “Causes of inflation” becomes “What are the main causes of inflation?”.
  3. Fill the answer from your notes or the recording, not from the slide — the slide is usually too thin.
  4. One idea per card. If an answer needs a list of six things, make it six cards or one card with a short list.
  5. Add the diagrams yourself. A text tool can’t read a diagram the way you can; describe it in a card (“What does the supply curve shift show?”).

What to check before you revise

Read every card once. AI can merge two ideas into one card, miss what a diagram showed or word a question so the answer is obvious. Delete or fix those — it takes a couple of minutes and you will trust the deck in the exam week.

Then actually use them

Go through the cards without looking at the answers, say the answer out loud, then flip. Put the ones you got wrong in a separate pile and repeat them tomorrow. Take the quiz at the end of the week as a check. That is the whole method — less re-reading, more remembering.
