# Why I Decided to Keep ParaFree Free Forever

Every so often someone asks me when I'm going to start charging for ParaFree, or when the "free trial" ends. The answer surprises them: the core tool is free, and I intend to keep it that way permanently. Not free-for-now, not free-until-I-build-an-audience-then-paywall-it. Actually free, for the basic features, for good.

I want to explain why I made that decision, because it wasn't just idealism. There's real reasoning behind it, and I think the reasoning is worth sharing whether you're building something yourself or just curious how a free tool is supposed to survive.

I'm Pritom Dash, an international student in Australia studying IT and cyber security, and ParaFree is a free paraphrasing tool I built and run on my own.

## The frustration that started it

ParaFree exists because I got tired of "free" tools that weren't really free.

You know the pattern. A tool advertises itself as free, you start using it, and then you hit a wall. A word limit at a hundred and twenty-five words. A "you've used your free quota" message. A download button that turns out to need an account, then a subscription. The word "free" was bait, and the actual product was locked behind a payment you only discovered once you were already invested.

I found that genuinely annoying as a user, and when I decided to build my own paraphrasing tool, I made a decision early: I wasn't going to do that to people. If I called it free, it would be free. No word limit that suddenly stops you mid-document. No fake free tier that's really a demo. The basic tool — paraphrasing your text, keeping your formatting, downloading the result — free, unlimited, for everyone.

## But how does a free tool survive?

This is the fair question, and I won't pretend free means the tool costs nothing to run. It costs my time, and eventually it will cost money to run at scale. So "free forever" only works if there's a real plan behind it. Here's mine, honestly.

The core tool stays free because that's the promise and the point. But "free basic features forever" doesn't have to mean "no way to ever earn anything from it." There's a difference between charging people to use the thing they came for, and offering extra things to people who want more.

My intention is to keep everything essential free, and later add optional extras on top — things like a higher-powered tier for heavy users who want more, or supporting the tool through advertising and relevant recommendations. The person who just wants to paraphrase a document keeps doing that for free, forever. The person who wants more can choose to pay for more. Nobody gets locked out of the basic promise.

That distinction matters to me. I'm not building a trap where the free version is deliberately crippled to force you to pay. I'm building a genuinely useful free tool, and letting the people who want extra capability fund it for everyone else.

## Why free is also a strategy, not just a value

I'll be honest that keeping it free isn't purely generous. It's also strategic, and I think it's worth understanding why.

Free removes the biggest barrier to someone trying your tool. There's no sign-up, no credit card, no "is this worth paying for" hesitation. Someone in any country, on any budget, can land on ParaFree and use it immediately. That's a huge part of why it's been able to reach people in India, Nigeria, the Philippines, Kenya, and other places where a paid tool would be a much harder sell.

Free also builds trust. When people discover a tool that actually delivers on "free, no limits, no catch," they remember it. They come back. They tell others. In a landscape full of tools that bait-and-switch, simply keeping your word is a competitive advantage.

So the free decision serves the users and the tool's growth at the same time. That alignment is rare and I try not to take it for granted.

## The commitment, plainly

I want to state it clearly, because I've written it down partly to hold myself to it: the basic features of ParaFree — paraphrasing, keeping your formatting, no word limit — will remain free for life. If I ever add paid options, they'll be genuine extras for people who want more, never a paywall around the core promise.

I'm a student building this with limited time and no budget, and I could probably make more money faster by locking features behind a subscription. I've chosen not to, because the entire reason ParaFree exists is that I was tired of tools that did exactly that. Building the thing I wished existed means keeping the promise that made me want to build it.

## If you're building something

If you're deciding how to price your own project, I'd offer this: think hard about the difference between charging for the core value and charging for extras on top of it. Free-forever for the essentials, with optional paid extras, is a real model — not charity, but a genuine strategy that builds trust and reach. It's slower to earn from than a hard paywall, but what it builds is more durable.

ParaFree is at parafree.app. It's free, there's no word limit, and there's no catch — which was the entire point.

— Pritom Dash
