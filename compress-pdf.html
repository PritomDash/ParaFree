Compress PDF to a Target Size

Shrink a PDF to fit an upload limit — pick 100 KB, 200 KB, 500 KB, 1 MB, 2 MB or type your own size. Free, no signup, no watermark, and you always see the size before and after.

🔒 No upload, no waiting — your file is processed on your device.

Last updated: October 22, 2026

How to use it

  1. Choose your PDF.
  2. Pick a mode: Light, Strong, or Target size with the limit you need.
  3. Click “Compress PDF” and read the result: the new size, and whether the text is still selectable.
  4. Download the compressed PDF.

Upload limits on university and job portals

Assignment portals, job application sites and scholarship forms often cap uploads at 1 MB, 2 MB or 5 MB, and a PDF full of photos or scans is easily bigger. Choose “Target size”, pick the limit and the tool does the rest.

It tries Light compression first, because that keeps your text selectable. If that is not small enough, it switches to Strong and searches for the highest quality that still fits under your target. Sizes use 1 MB = 1,000,000 bytes, as most upload pages count them.

Government and application form size limits

Online application forms often ask for scanned documents — certificates, ID pages, letters — under a small limit such as 100 KB, 200 KB or 500 KB. Pick the matching target. A one-page scan usually fits under 200 KB; many pages may not fit under a very small limit at a readable quality.

If a target cannot be reached, the tool says so plainly and offers the smallest version it could make, with its size. Then you can remove pages you don’t need, upload pages separately, or scan at a lower resolution.

There are step-by-step pages for the most common limits: getting a scanned form under 100 KB, getting a CV or application PDF under 200 KB and making a PDF fit a 500 KB upload limit.

Compress scanned PDFs

Scanned PDFs are big because every page is a photo. Light mode resizes those photos to about 150 DPI at the size they are shown — sharp on screen and in print — and saves them as efficient JPEG. That alone often makes a scanned PDF several times smaller.

Strong mode turns every page into one image at a lower resolution. That gives the smallest file, but the text can no longer be selected or searched.

If your “scan” is really a set of phone photos, turn the photos into one PDF first. Several documents for the same form can be merged into a single PDF and then compressed together.

Keep text selectable (Light)

Light mode changes images and nothing else. Text, links and vector graphics are left exactly as they were, so the text stays selectable and searchable — important for applicant tracking systems, similarity checkers and screen readers. If Light cannot make the file smaller, the tool tells you and offers your original instead.

Target size uses Light whenever Light is enough, and the result always tells you which method was used.

Compress a PDF on your phone

It works in the browser on Android and iPhone. Choose the PDF from your files or downloads, pick a target and download the smaller file — no app needed.

No upload, no waiting — your file is processed on your device. Large scanned PDFs take longer on a phone than on a laptop because the phone does the work.

Light, Strong and Target size — which to choose

Light keeps everything except oversized images, so the PDF looks the same and its text stays selectable. Use it first. Strong turns each page into one image: smallest file, but no selectable or searchable text. Target size chooses for you — Light if that is small enough, otherwise Strong at the highest quality that fits.

For a CV or anything read by an applicant tracking system, keep the text selectable: use Light, or a target that Light can reach. For scans and photo-heavy coursework, Strong or a target size is usually fine.

What it can and can’t do

It shrinks images and, if needed, rebuilds pages as images. It does not remove pages, fonts or attachments for you, and a PDF that is mostly text is often already as small as it can be — the tool tells you when that happens.

Very small targets for long documents may not be reachable at a readable quality; the tool stops at about 72 DPI rather than make pages unreadable, and tells you the smallest size it reached. No upload, no waiting — your file is processed on your device.

Frequently asked questions

Can I compress a PDF to exactly 100 KB or 200 KB?

You can set a target and the tool makes the file as good as possible while staying under it. It cannot always reach very small targets for long or detailed PDFs; then it tells you and offers the smallest version it made.

Will my text stay selectable?

Yes with Light mode, and with Target size whenever Light is enough. Strong mode turns pages into images, so the text is no longer selectable; the result always tells you which was used.

Is compressing a PDF free?

Yes. It is free with no signup and no watermark. With Light, files can be up to 2 GB and 2,000 pages on desktop Chrome or Edge (500 MB in other browsers); Strong and Target size handle up to 1 GB and 300 pages (500 MB in other browsers).

Is my PDF uploaded to a server?

No. No upload, no waiting — your file is processed on your device.

Why did my PDF not get smaller?

If a PDF is mostly text, or its images are already small, there is little to remove. The tool then says it is already well compressed and offers your original file.

How does Target size decide?

It tries Light first. If that is still above your target, it rebuilds the pages as images and searches for the highest resolution and quality that stays under the target, down to about 72 DPI. If even that is too big, you get the smallest version and a clear message.

Can I compress a PDF to under 100 KB on my phone?

Often, yes: choose Target size → 100 KB on the Compress PDF page in your phone’s browser. Text PDFs of a few pages usually fit; long scans may not, and the tool then tells you the smallest size it reached.

How do I reduce a PDF’s size without losing quality?

Use Light mode first: it shrinks large images to about 150 DPI and keeps text, vector graphics and fonts exactly as they were, so text stays sharp and selectable.

How do I compress a PDF for a job application?

Pick a target such as 200 KB or 1 MB. Light mode is tried first, so the text in your CV stays selectable for applicant tracking systems.
