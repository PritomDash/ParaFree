How to Make an ATS-Friendly CV That Recruiters’ Systems Can Read (Free, 2026)

By Pritom Dash · Published October 21, 2026

Many employers do not read your CV first — software does. An applicant tracking system (ATS) takes the file you upload, pulls the text out, and turns it into a record a recruiter can search: name, contact details, job titles, dates, skills. If the software cannot read your CV properly, a good application can look empty or jumbled. Here is how to avoid that.

What an ATS actually reads

An ATS reads the text in your file. It does not look at your design the way a person does. It looks for your name and contact details near the top, section headings it recognises (Experience, Education, Skills), job titles, employers and dates, and words that match the job advert. Then a recruiter searches and filters those records.

So an ATS-friendly CV is one whose text is real, in a sensible order, under clear headings. A beautiful design is fine — as long as the text underneath it is there and reads in the right order.

Image PDFs versus text PDFs

This is the most important and least known point. A PDF can contain real text, or it can contain a picture of text. They look identical on screen.

  • Text PDF: the letters are stored as text. You can select a word, copy it, search for it. An ATS can read it.
  • Image PDF: each page is one picture. Some CV builders and some “print to PDF” or screenshot routes produce this. There is no text to read unless the ATS runs OCR, and many do not — so your CV may come through as blank, or with recognition errors in your name or email.

How to check your PDF in ten seconds

  1. Open your CV PDF in a browser or PDF reader.
  2. Try to select a line of text with your mouse, or press Ctrl+A (Cmd+A on a Mac).
  3. Copy it and paste it into a plain text editor or an email.

If you can select the text and it pastes in a sensible order — name, contact details, then each section — an ATS can read it. If nothing gets selected, or the whole page is selected as one image, it is an image PDF. Rebuild it as a text PDF before you apply.

Layout choices that help

  • Use normal section headings: “Work Experience”, “Education”, “Skills”. Clever headings like “My Journey” are harder to recognise.
  • Put contact details in the body, not just in a page header or footer, which some systems skip.
  • Write dates consistently, such as “Jan 2023 – Present”.
  • Use the words from the advert where they are true for you — the software and the recruiter search for them.
  • Single-column templates are the safest choice for strict portals. Two-column designs work when their text is in a sensible order, which you can check with the copy-paste test.
  • Keep the file small. If a portal has a size limit, compress with a method that keeps text selectable — see compressing a PDF for upload limits.

One page or two?

For students and recent graduates, one page is usually right: it forces you to keep the strongest points, and recruiters read fast. Two pages are normal and fine for experienced roles, academic CVs or jobs that ask for detail. Three pages are rare outside academia and senior roles.

Word or PDF?

Upload whatever the employer asks for. If they accept either, a text PDF keeps your layout exactly as you designed it on every computer. ParaFree CVs download as PDF; cover letters download as PDF or Word.

A quick checklist

  • The text in your PDF can be selected and pastes in a sensible order.
  • Your name, email and phone are in the body of the CV.
  • Headings are plain: Experience, Education, Skills.
  • Dates are consistent and job titles are clear.
  • It is one page if you are a student or graduate, unless the role asks for more — and no page is just a few stray lines.
  • The file is under the portal’s size limit.
