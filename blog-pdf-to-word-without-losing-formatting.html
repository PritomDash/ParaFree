How to Convert PDF to Word Without Losing Formatting (Free, 2026)

You convert it to Word — and the result is a mess of broken lines, wrong fonts and columns mixed together. Here is why that happens, and how to get a Word file that actually looks like the PDF.

Why PDFs are hard to turn back into Word

A Word document knows its structure: this is a heading, this is a list, this is a table, this paragraph continues on the next page. A PDF mostly does not. It stores where each piece of text is drawn on the page — “these letters, in this font, at this position” — because its job is to look identical everywhere, not to be edited.

So a converter has to rebuild the structure from the layout. It has to work out which lines belong to one paragraph, which text is a heading because it is bigger and bold, which rows of text form a table, and in which order to read two columns. When a converter skips that work and copies line by line, you get the familiar broken result: a hard line break at the end of every line, headings that are just big normal text, and the left and right columns of a research paper woven together.

What a good conversion keeps

When you choose a PDF to Word converter, check that it keeps these:

  • Paragraphs, joined back together instead of one line per paragraph.
  • Headings as real Word headings, so the navigation pane and a table of contents work.
  • Bold, italic, font sizes and font families, at least where the PDF names its fonts.
  • Bullet and numbered lists as Word lists, not as lines that start with a symbol.
  • Tables as editable Word tables.
  • Two-column pages in reading order — left column first, then right.
  • Images in the right place.

Step by step

  1. Check what kind of PDF you have. Open it and try to select a sentence with your mouse. If you can, it is a text PDF and converts most accurately. If you can’t, it is a scan or a picture, and the converter needs OCR (see below).
  2. Convert it. Choose the PDF in the converter and wait while each page is read. Text pages are quick; scanned pages take longer.
  3. Read through the result. Look at the headings, lists and tables, and at the page breaks where paragraphs continue. Fix small things in the editor.
  4. Download the .docx and open it in Word, Google Docs or LibreOffice.

Fonts: why some look different

If the PDF uses a font that is not installed on your computer, Word shows a similar font instead. The size and style are still right, so the layout stays close, but lines can break in slightly different places. If the exact font matters, install it or pick a similar one in Word. This is the most common reason a converted document has one more or one fewer page than the PDF.

Two-column research papers

Journal articles are the classic failure case. A careless converter reads across the whole page, so the first line of the left column is followed by the first line of the right column. A good converter detects the columns and keeps each as normal paragraphs in reading order. Footnotes, captions and references come along as text. Complicated equations usually need tidying by hand in any converter.

Scanned PDFs need OCR

A scan has no text inside — every page is a photo. To get editable text, the converter has to read the letters with OCR (optical character recognition). ParaFree does this in your browser automatically when a page has no text layer. OCR is accurate on clear, straight, printed English text, and much weaker on handwriting, small print, coloured backgrounds and other languages, so proofread the result. A sharp, straight scan helps more than anything else.

What no converter does perfectly

Some PDFs are closer to posters than documents: text boxes placed over images, forms with fields, magazine-style layouts. No converter rebuilds these exactly, because the original structure simply is not in the PDF. For those, convert to get the text out, then lay it out again in Word.

Before you submit

Always open the Word file once from start to finish before you hand it in. Check headings, numbered lists, tables and references, and make sure nothing went missing at page breaks. If you edited the document and need a PDF again, convert it back with Word to PDF.
