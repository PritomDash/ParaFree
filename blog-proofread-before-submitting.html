A Proofreading Checklist Before You Submit an Essay

By Pritom Dash · Published November 4, 2026

Proofreading checklist?

A ten-minute proofreading checklist for essays and reports: the errors spell-check misses, how to read your own work, and how to use a grammar checker well.

I have lost marks for “there” instead of “their” in an essay I had read five times. You can’t proofread what you remember writing — your brain fills in what you meant. These are the tricks that actually catch mistakes.

Leave it, then change how it looks

Put the essay away for a few hours, even just overnight. Then change the font or the size, or read it on your phone. New-looking text gets read instead of remembered.

Read it out loud

Slow, every word. Missing words, repeated words (“the the”) and sentences that run on for ever are obvious when you hear them. Your device’s read-aloud feature does this for you.

The checklist

  • Homophones: their/there/they’re, its/it’s, affect/effect, than/then.
  • Agreement: “The results of the study were”, not “was”.
  • Apostrophes: “the student’s essay” (one), “the students’ essays” (many).
  • Tense: past for what you did (“we measured”), present for what is generally true.
  • Numbers and names: check every figure, date and author name against your source.
  • Citations: every in-text citation has a reference, and the other way round.
  • Formatting: headings consistent, page numbers, the right referencing style, the word count within the limit.

What should I check in the final two minutes?

Check the file name, the format the portal wants (PDF or Word) and that the right version is attached. If it has to be a PDF, export it from Word with Word to PDF so the text stays selectable.
