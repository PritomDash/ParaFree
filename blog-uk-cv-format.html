UK CV Format in 2026: Length, Layout and What to Leave Out

I moved countries for university and my first CV was the format from home: photo, date of birth, a whole page of “personal details”. In the UK that CV looked wrong before anyone read it. Here is the format that works here.

How long should a UK CV be?

Two A4 pages once you have some experience; one full page if you are a student or recent graduate. Recruiters skim, so the first half of page one does most of the work.

The usual section order

  1. Name and contact details — phone, email, town or city, LinkedIn. That’s all.
  2. Personal statement — three to five lines: who you are, your strongest skills, and the kind of role you want.
  3. Experience — most recent first; job title, employer, dates, then 3–5 bullet points that start with a verb and show a result.
  4. Education — most recent first; for graduates this can come before experience.
  5. Skills — the ones the job advert asks for, honestly.
  6. References — “available on request” is fine, or leave it out.

What to leave out

  • Photo — not expected in the UK (acting and modelling are the usual exceptions).
  • Date of birth and marital status — age is a protected characteristic under the Equality Act 2010; employers don’t need it.
  • Full home address — town or city is enough.
  • “Curriculum Vitae” as a title — your name is the title.

Writing bullet points that work

“Responsible for social media” says nothing. “Grew the society’s Instagram from 300 to 1,200 followers in one term” says a lot. If you don’t have a number, describe the outcome: what changed because you were there? Never invent figures — an interviewer will ask.

A UK CV layout you can copy

  1. Name and contact details — town or city, phone, email, LinkedIn. No photo, date of birth or full address.
  2. Personal profile — three or four lines: who you are, what you are looking for, one or two strengths with evidence.
  3. Education — most recent first: course, university, dates, expected or final grade, a relevant module or project if it helps.
  4. Work experience — most recent first, two to four bullet points each, starting with a verb and ending with a result where you can (“Cut stock-take time by a third by…”).
  5. Skills — languages (with level), software, certificates. Specific beats generic: “Excel pivot tables” rather than “IT skills”.
  6. Interests — optional; include them when they show something relevant.

Two pages is the usual limit; one page is fine for a student or graduate. Use British spelling, dates as “Sept 2024 – June 2025”, and send a PDF unless the employer asks for Word. You do not need to write “References available on request”.
